# P2 Pokémon Killing Ethics Contract

Status: **ACTIVE — P2 binding contract**  
Date: **2026-08-14**  
Decision: **P2-HDG-006 / D-021 / owner choice B**

## 1. Binding rule

Humans intentionally killing Pokémon is **contextual and culture/species-sensitive**.

The setting does not treat Pokémon killing as either a universal taboo or a universally ordinary harvest activity. Social and moral interpretation depends on the circumstances of the act and on the relationship between a particular community and a particular species.

Relevant factors include:

- immediate defense of self or others;
- settlement or route defense;
- necessary hunting for food or materials;
- mercy killing of a mortally injured creature;
- population or ecological control;
- scarcity and survival pressure;
- local species behavior and danger history;
- rarity or ecological importance;
- perceived intelligence, personhood, sacredness, omen status, or ancestral significance;
- local truces, protection customs, ritual obligations, or inherited grievances;
- whether non-lethal alternatives were realistically available;
- whether the act was necessary, wasteful, reckless, cruel, prestige-driven, or exploitative.

The same species may therefore be hunted in one community, tolerated in another, protected in a third, and treated as sacred or taboo elsewhere without violating the world contract.

## 2. Survival is legitimate, but not morally blank

The project permits humans to kill Pokémon where survival, defense, food, materials, mercy, or ecological necessity plausibly justify it.

This does **not** imply that every Pokémon encounter is a resource node or that a defeated Pokémon automatically becomes loot.

Later systems must distinguish at least these concepts:

- defeating or driving away a Pokémon;
- restraining or trapping it;
- injuring it;
- killing it in immediate defense;
- intentionally hunting it;
- killing it for food/materials;
- mercy killing;
- wasteful or prestige-only killing;
- killing a locally protected, sacred, rare, intelligent, bonded, or treaty-relevant Pokémon.

The social consequences of these actions may differ even when the physical outcome is the same.

## 3. No universal hunter morality

`hunter`, `tracker`, `watch`, `settlement defender`, and similar roles remain valid setting-native occupations.

However, the setting has no universal rule that hunters are heroic, shameful, sacred, or socially dominant. Their reputation is contextual:

- a hunter who removes a lethal threat may be celebrated;
- a hunter who wastes scarce animals or kills a protected species may be condemned;
- a specialist who understands when **not** to kill may be more valued than one who simply wins fights;
- a community may distinguish subsistence hunters, defenders, pest controllers, ritual hunters, trophy seekers, and poachers even if later terminology differs.

P3 may use these distinctions for backgrounds/reputation hooks, but should not create a generic `kill Pokémon for XP` progression loop.

## 4. Food and materials

Pokémon-derived food and materials are permitted where a species/local culture dossier justifies them.

P4 must not infer edible/useful harvest simply from a Pokémon being an animal-like creature. For every material use that matters, the species dossier should identify whether it is:

- directly source-backed;
- conservatively inferred from official evidence;
- explicitly project-authored local practice;
- intentionally absent or unresolved.

Non-lethal collection remains equally valid: shed material, abandoned nests, secretions, naturally lost parts, eggs only where appropriate, scavenged remains, negotiated access, or other species-specific methods may be preferable to killing.

## 5. Sacredness, intelligence, rarity, and local relationship

No single taxonomy such as `Legendary = sacred` or `smart Pokémon = never kill` is sufficient.

P4/P5 must evaluate concrete cases using source evidence plus local project lore. Relevant questions include:

- Does the community understand the species as unusually intelligent or communicative?
- Is it individually recognized rather than treated as an interchangeable animal population?
- Is it rare enough that killing one changes local ecology or culture?
- Does it guard, threaten, enable, or symbolize a local resource?
- Is there an inherited truce, debt, taboo, ritual, myth, or protection rule?
- Is the individual bonded to a human or settlement?

These factors can increase social consequences without creating a universal legal/moral hierarchy for all 151 species.

## 6. Companion rule

A companion Pokémon is not property and is not morally equivalent to an unrecognized wild encounter.

Because companionship represents an accepted ongoing relationship, deliberately killing a companion — one's own or another person's — is presumptively a severe social and narrative act unless an exceptional context such as unavoidable mercy killing clearly changes the interpretation.

Companion death remains governed by D-020: possible, real, and high-impact, but not routine attrition.

## 7. P4 species-dossier requirements

Every substantive Gen-I species dossier must include, where relevant:

- local human relationship categories that may differ by settlement;
- whether deliberate killing is ordinary, conditional, exceptional, taboo, or locally variable;
- valid survival/defense reasons for lethal force;
- food/material use or explicit `none`;
- non-lethal resource alternatives where meaningful;
- sacred/protected/truce/rare/intelligence factors where meaningful;
- likely consequences for reckless or culturally forbidden killing;
- evidence classification for any claimed use/taboo/practice.

`none` and `varies by locality` are valid authored results when justified; templates must not force every species into a hunting economy.

## 8. P5 event-engine/content requirements

P5 must be able to represent context-sensitive consequences rather than treating `kill` as a globally identical action.

Event/content state may need to distinguish:

- species and individual identity;
- location/community;
- reason/context of the killing;
- local knowledge known to the player;
- protected/truce/sacred/reputation flags;
- witnesses or discovered evidence;
- necessity versus avoidable/wasteful action;
- resulting reputation, grief, retaliation, ecological, resource, or quest consequences.

Exact schema fields belong to P5. This contract only requires that the design not collapse these meanings into a single universal morality flag.

## 9. Invariants

Later phases must preserve these rules unless the owner explicitly supersedes D-021:

1. Pokémon killing is neither universally forbidden nor universally routine.
2. Necessity and immediate defense can justify lethal action.
3. Local culture and species-specific relationships can materially change judgment.
4. One settlement may hunt a species another protects without creating a world contradiction.
5. Wasteful, reckless, cruel, or prestige-only killing may carry consequences even where subsistence hunting is accepted.
6. Defeat does not imply death, and death does not imply automatic resource harvesting.
7. Hunting occupations may exist without making the game a universal monster-harvest loop.
8. P4 owns species/local concrete treatment; P5 owns context-sensitive authored consequences.
9. Canon-first evidence boundaries remain mandatory for claimed Pokémon uses, biology, intelligence, or historical practices.
10. Companion killing is a severe relational act except where an exceptional context such as mercy killing clearly changes it.
