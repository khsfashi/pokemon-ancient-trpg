# World Content and Faction Contract

Status: **OWNER-APPROVED DIRECTION — implementation/content details deferred**  
Date: **2026-08-16**  
Applies to: **P5 validation, P8 vertical slice, P9 full content production**

## 1. Purpose

This project is not a Pokémon encounter checklist placed inside a generic medieval backdrop.

The intended game is a **pre-modern Pokémon world RPG**: ordinary people have debts, rivalries, duties, faith, fear, trade, crime, survival problems, political conflicts, mysteries, and long-running relationships, while the existence of Pokémon has materially shaped the society in which all of those things occur.

The binding design law is:

> **Every major world structure must be materially shaped by the existence of Pokémon, but every individual event does not need to visibly feature or be immediately caused by a Pokémon.**

A merchant dispute may simply be fraud. A missing person may have fled voluntarily. A bridge may fail because of poor maintenance. A feud may be human pride. Those events still take place in a world whose routes, economy, settlement boundaries, tools, taboos, beliefs, and power structures have developed under Pokémon ecology and danger.

## 2. Content pillars

The full authored game must contain substantive content in all of these pillars:

1. **Human / social** — family, debt, obligation, crime, trade, rivalry, leadership, trust, betrayal, care, negotiation, punishment, reconciliation.
2. **Survival / travel** — weather, hunger, injury, route loss, shelter, damaged infrastructure, dangerous crossings, supply failure, rescue.
3. **Exploration / mystery** — ruins, abandoned sites, old boundaries, strange records, lost settlements, unexplained phenomena, partial truths.
4. **Relationship / consequence** — remembered choices, NPC trust, local reputation, obligations, favors, grudges, recurring characters.
5. **Faction / long arc** — persistent organizations, ideological conflict, influence across localities, internal divisions, cross-faction consequences.
6. **Pokémon ecology / direct interaction** — habitat, danger, observation, direct encounters, coexistence, conflict, exceptional bonds, rare/singular encounters.
7. **Mixed world events** — human motives and Pokémon ecology affect one another without collapsing into a single explanation.

No pillar is a separate gameplay engine. They must all use the P5 event/condition/choice/check/consequence model.

## 3. Pokémon-shaped world rule

For a durable institution, faction, trade network, belief system, military practice, craft tradition, criminal economy, settlement custom, or long-running conflict to enter canon, the author must be able to answer:

> **What is materially different about this structure because Pokémon exist?**

Valid answers can involve:

- danger and settlement defense;
- migration and route access;
- species-specific ecology;
- sacredness, taboo, omen, or ritual interpretation;
- rare companionship or negotiated coexistence;
- Pokémon-dependent technology islands;
- medicine, materials, food, transport, labor, or environmental effects where the relevant P2/P4 contracts permit them;
- knowledge monopolies and identification expertise;
- military or coercive attempts to control access to Pokémon-dependent power;
- protection, exploitation, poaching, trafficking, habitat conflict, or ecological stewardship;
- Psychic/Ghost/anomaly phenomena;
- legendary/mythical beliefs and exceptional events;
- another explicit Pokémon-world causal bridge consistent with existing contracts.

`Generic medieval organization + Pokémon-themed name/logo` is insufficient.

## 4. Major faction requirement

P9 full-world content must establish **several persistent major factions**. The current production target is approximately **4–6 distinct major faction lanes**.

This number is a planning range, not a requirement to invent exactly six final organizations immediately. Exact count, names, symbols, leaders, territorial reach, and final doctrines remain future Human Design Gate material before expensive P9 production if multiple strong directions remain.

A major faction must satisfy all of the following:

- recur across more than one isolated event;
- affect people, routes, settlements, resources, beliefs, knowledge, or Pokémon relationships;
- have a Pokémon-shaped causal foundation;
- expose at least one meaningful player-facing choice or consequence;
- remain compatible with the pre-regional-civilization setting;
- not require a modern Kanto-wide bureaucracy, nation-state, trainer institution, or standardized infrastructure;
- be representable through ordinary P5 state/events rather than bespoke faction code.

A major faction may therefore be a distributed shrine network, cult, lineage confederation, warrior alliance, merchant web, smuggling network, lorekeeper network, ecological movement, or another persistent pre-modern structure.

## 5. Approved faction design lanes

The following are **approved design lanes**, not yet final named canon factions. P9 may combine, split, or reject individual lanes through later explicit design work as long as the final faction set preserves the contract above.

### 5.1 Sacred / religious network

A network that interprets certain Pokémon, habitats, migrations, Psychic/Ghost phenomena, or legendary events as sacred, dangerous, revelatory, or ritually significant.

It may provide real social goods such as refuge, healing, mediation, burial, warnings, or preserved knowledge while also containing doctrinal conflict, political interests, or extremist branches.

It must not become a generic fantasy church whose Pokémon connection is decorative.

### 5.2 Coercive political / military / utilitarian faction

A faction that treats Pokémon-dependent capability, territory, migration, or knowledge as strategic power.

Because portable containment does not exist, control cannot default to modern capture infrastructure. Coercion may instead involve habitat seizure, food/water control, nesting-site pressure, forced labor arrangements, dangerous restraint, monopolized specialists, hostage-like ecological leverage, or control of access to a technology island.

This faction can provide security or infrastructure as well as abuse power; it need not be uniformly evil.

### 5.3 Ecological protection / human-withdrawal movement

A faction that believes human expansion, hunting, extraction, settlement, road building, or specific practices are damaging Pokémon ecology or violating an inherited relationship.

Moderate members may negotiate sustainable coexistence. Extremists may accept severe human costs to restore or protect habitat.

### 5.4 Lorekeeper / research / anomaly network

A distributed set of scholars, record keepers, ritual specialists, observers, healers, or investigators who preserve difficult knowledge about species, ruins, rare phenomena, chronology anomalies, or dangerous ecological patterns.

They may disagree over whether knowledge should be public, restricted, weaponized, hidden, or destroyed.

This lane is especially suitable for exceptional chronology-anomaly content but must not create a modern scientific institution or ancient computer industry.

### 5.5 Illicit exploitation / hunting / trafficking network

A criminal or semi-legal network built around valuable Pokémon-related information, habitat access, eggs where permitted, dangerous materials, shed resources, rare medicines, trophies, territorial clearing, smuggling, or similar exploitation.

It must respect D-021/P4 species-specific killing/material rules and must not create generic Pokémon loot drops.

Ordinary crimes such as extortion, bribery, kidnapping, fraud, route robbery, and market manipulation can connect to this network without a Pokémon appearing in every scene.

### 5.6 Secretive transformative / apocalyptic cult

A possible long-arc antagonist lane whose doctrine is rooted in a distorted conclusion about the relationship between humans and Pokémon.

Examples of the *type* of doctrine that could fit include forced unity, absolute hierarchy, purification, return to a pre-human ecological order, or an attempt to interpret/trigger a legendary or anomalous phenomenon.

No exact doctrine, legendary Pokémon, prophecy, or final-boss structure is frozen by this contract. Those are later Human Design Gate decisions.

## 6. Moral and internal complexity

The world must not expose all faction politics as a simple `GOOD / EVIL` alignment chart.

At least some major factions should have:

- useful public functions;
- sympathetic members;
- internal factions or doctrinal disagreement;
- legitimate grievances;
- methods that can be defended in one context and condemned in another.

Truly malicious actors, predatory networks, extremist branches, or openly destructive cults are allowed. Moral complexity does not require pretending every faction is equally justified.

Player consequences should arise from concrete actions, relationships, reputation, permissions, obligations, information, and local conditions rather than one universal morality meter.

## 7. Event causality rule

Authors must avoid the pattern:

> problem appears → Pokémon is revealed as the cause → solve Pokémon problem

as the default structure for ordinary content.

Valid event causality includes:

- human cause, no Pokémon involvement;
- environmental cause, with Pokémon only part of the background ecology;
- Pokémon cause, human conflict over how to respond;
- human exploitation causing Pokémon/ecological consequences;
- apparent Pokémon cause that is actually human action;
- apparent human dispute that reveals an ecological or Pokémon-dependent constraint;
- multiple causes with no single clean villain.

The goal is a world where Pokémon matter structurally without becoming the answer to every mystery.

## 8. P5 requirements

Before P5 closes, the event contract/validation evidence must prove that one shared data-driven surface can represent:

- a human/social event whose immediate cause is not Pokémon;
- a survival/environmental event;
- a persistent relationship or faction-linked chain;
- a Pokémon ecological/direct-interaction event;
- a mixed human/Pokémon event;
- faction/relationship state changes through typed owner-domain adapters rather than raw upstream writes.

P5 does **not** need production faction names, lore, or a full reputation subsystem if the existing semantic state/commands can express the required relationships and permissions. P5 remains implementation-neutral.

## 9. P8 requirements

The first playable vertical slice must demonstrate a mixed loop rather than a Pokémon-only demo.

One coherent run must include at least:

- settlement/social pressure;
- one ordinary human/social event not immediately caused by a Pokémon;
- one survival/travel event;
- one faction-linked clue, pressure, relationship consequence, or recurring thread;
- one Pokémon ecology/direct-interaction event;
- one point where human motives and Pokémon ecology intersect;
- a valid `0/3` companion path.

The full major-faction roster is not required in P8. One faction thread or credible foreshadowing is enough to prove the pipeline.

## 10. P9 requirements

P9 is **Full World Content Production**, not merely the phase that fills a 151-row Pokémon checklist.

P9 must deliver both:

### A. Pokémon realization

- all National Pokédex #001-#151 have at least one authored **living direct-interaction path** somewhere in total content under D-034;
- indirect traces, rumors, myths, bestiary records, dreams, or environmental signs are valuable but do not substitute for direct encounter coverage;
- direct encounter does not imply capture, companionship, combat, natural/common spawn, one-run availability, or equal frequency.

### B. World realization

- substantial human/social event breadth;
- settlement/economy/trade pressure;
- survival and travel;
- exploration and mystery;
- relationship/reputation/obligation consequences;
- several recurring major factions and long-running faction chains;
- mixed human/Pokémon world events;
- endings and long arcs that can depend on these systems without requiring a Pokémon companion.

P9 closure therefore needs **both** a species direct-encounter manifest and a world-content/faction coverage audit.

## 11. Performance and architecture boundary

This contract creates content requirements, not permission for continuous simulation.

- faction/event eligibility remains transition-driven under P5;
- no per-frame faction scan;
- no per-frame all-event scan;
- no whole-151 roster scan to decide ordinary events;
- faction state should use stable indexed/typed state rather than repeatedly deriving history from an unbounded log;
- P7 owns storage/runtime implementation after P6.

## 12. Deferred Human Design Gates

This contract intentionally does **not** freeze:

- exact final number of major factions inside the 4–6 planning range;
- faction names, heraldry, leaders, headquarters, or map placement;
- which lane becomes the primary antagonist;
- which Legendary/Mythical Pokémon, if any, anchors a major cult or endgame conflict;
- whether one faction can be joined formally or only influenced;
- exact reputation UI/mechanics beyond the requirement for persistent consequences;
- final endings attached to factions.

Those choices should be asked only when needed for production authoring, after the relevant P5/P6/P7 constraints are known.
