# P2 Lethality and Death Canon Research

Status: **P2 research input; not itself a design decision**  
Last updated: **2026-08-14**

## 1. Question

Before freezing the game's injury/death/severity tone, determine what official Pokémon material actually supports about:

- wild Pokémon threatening humans;
- Pokémon death;
- humans killing or hunting Pokémon;
- whether ordinary battle defeat should be interpreted as death;
- how far this project can intensify ancient danger without presenting project-authored grimness as canon.

This document follows D-018: **official evidence → bounded interpretation → explicit project extrapolation**.

## 2. Primary official evidence

### E-LETH-001 — Wild Pokémon can directly attack people

Source: official Pokémon Legends: Arceus gameplay page, The Pokémon Company International.

The official gameplay description states that wild Pokémon react differently to people and that some species come directly at the player and attack. Alpha Pokémon are described as chasing and attacking the player when noticed, and the page explicitly warns the player about getting into danger.

**Classification:** direct official fact.

**What this supports:**

- Pokémon danger to an unprotected human is not merely fan interpretation;
- physical avoidance and retreat are legitimate Pokémon-world behaviors;
- an earlier, less integrated era can plausibly make direct human exposure more dangerous.

**What this does not prove:**

- that attacks normally kill humans;
- exact injury rates;
- that every aggressive Pokémon fights to the death.

### E-LETH-002 — Even Hisui treats ordinary human–Pokémon coexistence as uncommon

Source: official Pokémon Legends: Arceus gameplay page.

The official page says that people living side by side with Pokémon are rare in Hisui and people who lead Pokémon in battle are rarer still.

**Classification:** direct official fact.

**What this supports:**

- the project's still-earlier social baseline can legitimately intensify fear, avoidance and lack of trainer-style safety norms;
- trained companion intervention should not be assumed as the ordinary answer to dangerous wildlife.

### E-LETH-003 — Pokémon death, graves and an afterlife are explicitly present in official Kanto material

Source: official Pokémon 25th-anniversary Kanto retrospective on Pokémon.com discussing Lavender Town and Pokémon Tower.

The official retrospective describes Pokémon Tower as containing graves of many deceased Pokémon. It also recounts the Kanto story in which a Marowak loses its life protecting its child and its restless spirit is encountered before departing to the afterlife.

**Classification:** direct official fact.

**What this supports:**

- Pokémon can die rather than universally converting all harm into harmless fainting;
- burial, mourning, spirits and cultural practices around Pokémon death are franchise-compatible;
- companion or wild Pokémon death may exist in this project without contradicting the franchise at a foundational level.

**What this does not prove:**

- that standard battle defeat normally means death;
- that death is common in modern trainer battles;
- that every Ghost encounter is a dead Pokémon.

### E-LETH-004 — Humans have hunted Pokémon severely enough to threaten a population

Source: official Pokémon animation episode page for `So Near, Yet So Farfetch'd` on Pokémon.com.

The official synopsis states that excessive hunting by humans left very few Farfetch'd in existence, making the species rare and valuable.

**Classification:** direct official fact for the official animated continuity represented by that page.

**What this supports:**

- human hunting of Pokémon is an explicit franchise precedent;
- human pressure can materially affect Pokémon populations;
- the project may consider hunting, poaching, resource conflict and conservation as species-specific social relationships.

**What this does not prove:**

- that every Pokémon species is ordinarily hunted;
- that hunting is morally or culturally accepted everywhere;
- that the project should make Pokémon meat or killing a generic crafting resource.

## 3. Cross-evidence synthesis

The evidence establishes all of the following without requiring project invention:

1. wild Pokémon can attack humans directly;
2. human–Pokémon coexistence was already uncommon in the official historical Hisui comparator;
3. Pokémon can truly die and be mourned/buried;
4. humans can hunt Pokémon and can pressure a species through that hunting.

Therefore a dangerous ancient TRPG where injury, death, hunting and defensive killing are **possible** is compatible with official Pokémon material.

However, the same evidence does not establish that:

- ordinary battles are routinely lethal;
- all wild Pokémon intend to kill;
- all human communities treat Pokémon as meat/resources;
- fainting and death are the same state;
- grimdark violence is the franchise default.

Those remain project design choices.

## 4. Recommended project boundary

The project should preserve three distinct outcome concepts:

### 4.1 Repelled / escaped / disengaged

The encounter ends without either side being fully incapacitated.

This should be common because the existing world contract already treats avoidance, detours, terrain use, appeasement and retreat as valid encounter resolutions.

### 4.2 Incapacitated / unable to continue

A human or Pokémon can be too injured, exhausted, stunned or otherwise compromised to continue a conflict without necessarily being dead.

This concept preserves familiar Pokémon battle readability without pretending all serious harm is harmless.

### 4.3 Dead

Death is a separate terminal state and should never be silently inferred from every `defeat` result.

If P3 later permits permanent death, the event/rules contract should make the transition into death explicit enough that authors and players can distinguish it from incapacitation.

## 5. Why `every defeat = death` is not recommended

The project wants Pokémon to feel monster-scale dangerous, but automatically treating ordinary battle loss as death would create several problems:

- it would erase a useful distinction already present in Pokémon's battle language;
- it would make every companion fight implicitly a death match;
- it would pressure a no-companion run and a companion run into very different moral games;
- it would make P5 event authors accidentally kill species or NPCs through routine combat resolution;
- it would turn P4 ecology into a generic loot/hunting framework unless heavily counter-designed.

A serious setting does not need universal lethality to make a Mach-speed, poisonous, psychic or massive Pokémon terrifying.

## 6. Why `death never happens` is also not recommended

A blanket non-lethal rule would conflict with the intended ancient-monster tone and unnecessarily ignore direct franchise precedent for Pokémon death, graves and human hunting.

It would also weaken consequences around:

- remote travel;
- settlement defense;
- hunting/poaching conflicts;
- dangerous species ecology;
- companion protection;
- catastrophic event outcomes;
- funerary or spiritual traditions.

## 7. Design implication for the next Human Design Gate

The strongest candidate is a **serious selective-lethality** model:

- ordinary encounters do not default to death;
- injury and incapacitation are real;
- severe or permanent injury may occur under clearly dangerous circumstances;
- death is possible for humans and Pokémon but is a separate consequence, not the synonym for defeat;
- the world can contain hunting, defensive killing and deliberate lethal violence where culture/ecology supports it;
- exact ethics and prevalence of killing Pokémon should be resolved as a follow-up gate rather than silently generalized to all species;
- companion death, if permitted mechanically, should be treated as a high-impact event rather than routine attrition unless the owner explicitly chooses a much harsher game.

This recommendation is a project design inference, not an official franchise rule.

## 8. Candidate P2-HDG-005 — severity baseline

### A — Adventure-safe

- routine injury is recoverable;
- permanent injury is exceptional/scripted;
- death is mostly special narrative content or explicit endings;
- combat defeat generally means incapacitation/retreat.

**Benefit:** accessible and forgiving.  
**Cost:** weakest fit for the owner's dangerous ancient-monster premise.

### B — Serious selective lethality **(recommended)**

- injury is consequential and can affect the remainder of a run;
- serious/permanent injury is possible from major failures or dangerous encounters;
- death is possible but is an explicit high-severity outcome rather than the default result of losing a fight;
- humans cannot casually trade hits with Pokémon just because the game uses RPG combat;
- retreat, preparation and avoidance remain core survival tools;
- companion death, if later implemented, is rare/high-impact rather than routine attrition.

**Benefit:** preserves real danger and meaningful survival choices without turning every encounter into grimdark roulette.

### C — Harsh lethal survival

- severe injury and death are common consequences of combat failure;
- permanent character loss and companion loss are ordinary run risks;
- many direct battles become effectively life-or-death decisions.

**Benefit:** strongest monster-survival tone.  
**Cost:** high frustration risk, can make experimentation/companionship unattractive, and may dominate the intended event-driven adventure with attrition management.

## 9. Deferred follow-up after severity

After P2-HDG-005, separately decide the **social/ethical treatment of killing Pokémon**:

- when hunting is accepted;
- whether meat/material harvesting is species-specific;
- defensive killing norms;
- taboo/sacred species;
- whether killing a companion-capable or sapient-seeming species is treated differently;
- settlement law/custom and reputation consequences.

That gate should remain species- and culture-sensitive rather than declaring one global moral answer for all 151 species.
