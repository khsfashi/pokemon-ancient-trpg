# P2 Canon Derivation and Pokémon-Dependent Technology Contract

Status: **BINDING P2 CONTRACT**  
Date: **2026-08-14**

This document freezes how project-authored ancient-world lore is derived from Pokémon canon and how Pokémon capabilities may produce uneven local technology without turning the setting into a hidden modern civilization.

It operationalizes owner-approved P2 direction and must constrain P4 species dossiers and later authored content.

## 1. Core world rule

The ordinary human material baseline is **iron-age / medieval-fantasy-like**.

On top of that common floor, individual settlements, clans, lineages, workshops, shrines, healers, or specialists may possess **Pokémon-dependent technology islands**: a narrow craft or practice that exceeds what neighboring communities can do because generations of people learned around a particular Pokémon capability, ecology, material, or recurring phenomenon.

A technology island is an exception layered onto the iron-age baseline. It is not evidence of a general modern scientific or industrial technology tree.

## 2. What counts as a technology island

A technology island must satisfy all of the following:

1. **Pokémon causal anchor** — the enabling ability, material, behavior, ecology, or phenomenon must be traceable to official Pokémon evidence or a clearly marked derivation from it.
2. **Human craft bridge** — the setting must explain what humans actually learned, built, prepared, harvested, timed, insulated, shaped, preserved, redirected, or organized around that phenomenon.
3. **Narrow domain** — the result advances one bounded craft or practice, not civilization in general.
4. **Local dependency** — access depends on a species, habitat, migration, material source, inherited specialist knowledge, dangerous site, negotiated relationship, or another concrete local condition.
5. **Premodern scalability** — the practice must remain limited by labor, transport, knowledge transmission, dangerous Pokémon access, material scarcity, reliability, or social structure.
6. **No ownership shortcut** — the enabling Pokémon does not need to be a tame companion, and the existence of a technology island must never imply a routine obedient Pokémon workforce.

Examples of valid causal shapes include:

- observing a recurring Pokémon phenomenon and building a craft process around its timing;
- safely collecting shed, abandoned, secreted, or naturally occurring Pokémon-derived material;
- maintaining distance-based coexistence with a species whose behavior benefits a settlement;
- using a rare bonded Pokémon in a specialist practice where that bond itself remains exceptional;
- constructing equipment specifically to resist, redirect, measure, contain, survive, or exploit one known Pokémon phenomenon.

## 3. What does not count

The following are prohibited unless a later explicit owner decision supersedes this contract:

- `Electric-type exists, therefore the settlement has a modern electrical grid`;
- `Fire-type exists, therefore metallurgy automatically jumps to industrial steelmaking`;
- `Psychic-type exists, therefore telecommunication or computation is solved`;
- mass farms of captured Pokémon functioning as standardized machinery;
- an elemental move being treated as proof that humans understand the corresponding modern science;
- one local craft automatically spreading everywhere without route, knowledge, ecology, and social-friction consequences;
- adding advanced technology only because it would be mechanically convenient.

Pokémon powers can change the **shape** of technological history without erasing the project's premodern society.

## 4. Canon-first derivation protocol

Ancient-setting lore must be built in this order:

> **official evidence → bounded interpretation → explicit project extrapolation**

Content authors may not reverse the process by inventing a desired fantasy result first and then searching for a Pokédex sentence that can be stretched to justify it.

Every material species claim should be classified into one of these evidence layers:

### C1 — Direct official fact

Explicitly stated in official game text, Pokédex text, official game/series material, or another rights-holder-published source.

Example shape: `the source explicitly says this species can do X`.

### C2 — Cross-source official synthesis

Not stated in one sentence, but follows directly from multiple compatible official facts without adding a new fictional mechanism.

Example shape: `official facts A and B jointly establish that this phenomenon is real and relevant`.

### D1 — Conservative derived inference

A project inference that is a natural consequence of C1/C2 evidence and the already-approved ancient-world constraints.

Example shape: `if local people repeatedly survive around X, they plausibly learn a bounded safety practice against X`.

### D2 — Project-authored extrapolation

A creative setting decision inspired by canon but not established by it.

Example shape: `this village developed a hereditary smithing ritual around that phenomenon`.

D2 content is allowed and expected. It must simply remain labeled as project-authored rather than being written as Pokémon canon.

### U — Unsupported / unresolved

A proposed claim without sufficient evidence or owner approval. It cannot become binding lore until researched, narrowed, or approved.

## 5. Source discipline for P4

Every one of the 151 mandatory Generation-I dossiers must separate **what Pokémon canon says** from **what this project imagines**.

At minimum, each dossier must contain:

- `official_evidence` — the relevant official or version-tagged factual basis;
- `evidence_class` — C1 / C2 / D1 / D2 / U as applicable;
- `ancient_ecology_derivation` — how the ancient interpretation follows from the evidence;
- `human_perception_culture` — project treatment of how people understand the species;
- `technology_craft_hook` — a sourced or derived local craft consequence, or explicit `none`;
- `ancient_strength_treatment` — stronger / similar / behaviorally harsher / otherwise altered / unchanged / unresolved, with provenance;
- `companionship_treatment` — whether and under what exceptional conditions the species can occupy one of the three companion slots;
- `canon_boundary_note` — any point likely to be mistaken for official lore.

A species dossier is not improved by forcing every species to produce a technology. `technology_craft_hook: none` is a valid and often preferable result.

## 6. Ancient-strength rule remains species-specific

The project wants ancient Pokémon to be frightening enough that iron weapons do not produce human–Pokémon parity.

Canon provides at least species-level precedent for long-term strength change, but there is no universal official rule that civilization genetically weakened every Pokémon.

Therefore P4 must determine the ancient treatment species by species. Acceptable outcomes include:

- physically stronger ancient population;
- larger or more robust ancient population;
- similar physical capability but much more aggressive behavior;
- same species strength but harsher ecological conditions making encounters deadlier;
- local ancient lineage stronger while other populations are not;
- no meaningful ancient-versus-modern difference;
- a canon-supported historical change unique to that species.

The project may later adopt a broader civilization-driven weakening theory as **project lore**, but it must never be presented as a franchise-wide canonical fact.

## 7. Human psychics and anomalous people

Rare human psychics / miracle-workers are permitted under D-015 and belong to the same canon-first discipline.

They can fill part of the narrative and mechanical niche occupied by fantasy magic-users, but:

- there is no generic learnable magic system;
- powers are narrow personal faculties, not interchangeable spell lists;
- exceptional power should make an NPC or PC socially unusual;
- local culture may interpret the same phenomenon as blessing, curse, omen, lineage gift, spirit sensitivity, or another premodern explanation;
- P3 owns mechanical limits and costs.

Pokémon-derived technology must not silently turn a rare psychic into a reusable machine component.

## 8. Design consequences

This contract intentionally creates an uneven world.

Two settlements at similar population and wealth may differ dramatically because one inherited a rare local practice and the other did not. A lost migration route, dead specialist, hostile Pokémon population, ecological collapse, feud, or destroyed workshop can erase a technology island within a generation.

This supports:

- meaningful travel and regional identity;
- specialist occupations and lineage knowledge;
- discoveries that genuinely change a run;
- rare trade goods and expertise;
- settlements with distinct survival strategies;
- Pokémon ecology affecting human history without requiring routine ownership;
- source-driven P4 worldbuilding rather than generic medieval reskinning.

## 9. Cross-phase requirements

### P3

Rules may model specialist knowledge, tools, backgrounds, rare psychic gifts, and access to local techniques, but must not assume modern universal infrastructure.

### P4

All 151 dossiers must use the evidence-layer protocol and explicitly audit technology/craft hooks and ancient-strength treatment.

### P5

Events should be able to test whether the player knows, lacks, learns, trades for, loses, or misapplies local technology rather than treating it as a permanent global unlock.

### P6

Resource/art direction should visually support iron-age common material culture plus rare local anomalies without making the overall world look industrial or modern.

### P7-P9

Data structures and content pipelines must preserve provenance/evidence classes so project-authored extrapolation cannot later be mistaken for imported canon data.
